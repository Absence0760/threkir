## A time trial that is always open

A park course is a fixed course on a trail in a state or national park, with a leaderboard that never closes. There is no organiser, no entry fee and no race day. You run it whenever the park is open, on your own or with friends, and your time goes on the board.

That makes it a **time trial, not a race**. The trail stays open to everyone and nobody marshals it, so the usual trail manners apply: follow the park's rules, give way to hikers and riders, and be ready for the conditions.

## How a run gets its time

Any run with a GPS track can count, whether you recorded it with Threkir or imported it from another app. The run is compared with the course line:

- **Start and finish.** The clock starts where your run crosses the course start and stops where it crosses the finish. Running to the trailhead and back doesn't count against you.
- **Checkpoints.** Many courses have checkpoints along the way. Your run must pass every one of them, in order, to get a time.
- **The whole course.** A run that covers only part of the course, or a much shorter line, gets no time.

## Why checkpoints matter

A checkpoint sits on the far side of every corner someone could cut: a switchback, a junction, a path across the middle of a loop. Miss one and the run isn't timed, so a shortcut can never put anyone at the top of the board.

It also protects the trail. Cutting switchbacks causes erosion, and parks treat it as a breach of their rules. **Stay on the marked trail.**

## Seasons follow the park's clock

Each board has a season: the calendar year **in the park's own time zone**. The boards start again at midnight on 1 January where the park is, whatever time zone your phone is in. A run that starts at 22:00 on 31 December in a Virginia park counts for that year.

A new season every January gives everyone a fresh chance at a placing, and the all-time board keeps the records.

## Two boards, two ways to win

- **Fastest** ranks each runner by their best time, once per runner. Switch between this year and all time, and filter by gender or age band.
- **Most completions** counts the days you completed the course in the season. Several laps on one day count as one, so it rewards the regulars who come back week after week rather than one long day of laps.

Private runs stay private: a time from a run other people can't see isn't shown to them.
